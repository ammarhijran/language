# Lisan — vocabulary & pronunciation

A language-learning app focused on **vocabulary** and **pronunciation** in four languages: **Turkish 🇹🇷, Ukrainian 🇺🇦, Japanese 🇯🇵 and Arabic 🇸🇦**.

It runs as a website (works offline, installable as a PWA) and as native **Android** and **iOS** apps built from the same code with [Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com). No account is needed, and your progress is saved on your device and never sent to us.

## Features

### Getting around
- **Five tabs:** Home (today: goal ring, one "what next" button, shortcuts, word of the day, weekly challenges) · Learn (Words, Phrases, Talk like a local, Grammar, Read & Listen) · Practice (reviews, modes, sentences, speaking and ear training) · Phrasebook · Me (progress, goals, settings, sync).
- **Topic pages** list every entry with its reading, register, audio and ★, with Learn next 5 / Practise / Sentences / Say them.
- **Phrasebook:** 220+ ready-to-use phrases in 22 situations, searchable, with a polite ⇄ everyday switch (MSA ⇄ Egyptian in Arabic), ★ favourites (synced between devices) and a big-text **Show** mode for showing your phone to someone.
- **Search** (top bar) finds words, phrases, topics, grammar lessons and texts by script, reading or English.
- **Speaking as a woman:** in Ukrainian and Arabic, “I …” phrases that depend on the speaker’s gender can be shown, spoken and checked in the feminine form (Settings → Display).
- **My words:** add your own words and phrases to a language and learn, review and practise them like the course’s (kept on the device, in backups and in sync codes).
- **My note:** write your own memory hook on any word; it shows up after every answer about it.
- **Themes:** light or dark (or whatever the device uses), eight colours for buttons and links — seven presets or any hue of your own — and five page backgrounds, including true black for OLED screens. Every combination is checked for readable contrast.
- **Display settings:** larger text for the whole app, and reading aids you can turn down as you learn the script — hide rōmaji (kana only) or all readings in Japanese, or the transliteration in Arabic. New words and answers always show the full reading.
- **Daily reminder** (Android and iOS apps): one notification a day at the time you choose, saying what's waiting ("12 Turkish words are ready for review"), skipped once you've met your goal. Scheduled on the device; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Continue where you left off:** the app remembers your topic, and the learning path mixes word topics, phrase situations and everyday speech. Leaving a lesson half-way keeps the words you've seen.
- **Focused lessons:** sessions run full screen; after each answer a bottom sheet shows right/wrong with the answer, and Continue is always in reach.

### Vocabulary
- **77 topics per language, chosen by everyday frequency**, in three groups:
  - **Words** (44 topics, 781–913 words): Essentials, Numbers, People & Family, Food & Drink, Time & Days, Colors & Descriptions, Places & Travel, Home & Things, Body & Health, Common Verbs, Questions & Little Words, Nature & Animals, More Everyday Verbs, Daily Routine, Feelings & Opinions, Shopping & Money, Connecting Words, Work & Study, Months & Dates, Family & Relationships, Clothes, Free Time & Hobbies, Countries & Languages, Weather, Fruit/Vegetables & Groceries, Doctor & Pharmacy, Phone & Internet, Around Town, Kitchen & Cooking, Jobs, Animals, School & Study, Money & Bank, Eating Out, Sports & Fitness, Arts & Entertainment, Personality & People, More Useful Verbs, Housework & Repairs, Cars & Driving, Holidays & Celebrations, News & Media, Landscape & Environment and More Describing Words.
  - **Phrases** (31 situations, about 355 ready-to-use sentences): Small Talk, Café & Restaurant, Shopping, Getting Around, Making Plans, Reactions, Problems & Help, Polite Requests, Language Help, Introducing Yourself, At Someone's Home, Wishes & Occasions, Doctor & Pharmacy, Phone & Messages (including real texting forms), Hotel, Emergencies, Trains/Planes/Taxis, At Work, Opinions, Friends & Compliments, At a Restaurant, Renting & Home, Bank/Post & Paperwork, Airport & Border, Problems & Complaints, Feelings & Support, Handy Extras, Sightseeing & Tickets, Dates/Times & Appointments, Hairdresser/Repairs & Services, Getting to Know People.
  - **Talk like a local** (2 topics, 45 entries): everyday spoken forms and slang — Turkish *naber, kanka, aynen*; Ukrainian *норм, класно*; Japanese *やばい, マジで, めっちゃ*; Egyptian Arabic *يلا، خلاص، معلش*.
  Most entries have an example sentence and a usage note.
- **Trip essentials** in the Phrasebook: the 37–41 things to be able to say first (hello, thank you, “I don’t understand”, “Where is the toilet?”, “How much is this?”, “I need a doctor”…), gathered from all topics — to learn as a short crash course, practise or play hands-free.
- **Polite vs everyday speech, side by side.** Words and phrases carry a register (formal, polite, neutral, casual, slang) and, where people say it differently, the other version: *"What people say"* next to *"Proper / polite"* (in Arabic: the Egyptian everyday form next to Modern Standard Arabic). Both sides can be played.
- **Grammar:** 16 short lessons per language (A1 → B1), each with explanations, tables, playable examples and a 4-question quiz — from Turkish vowel harmony, Ukrainian cases and Japanese particles to Arabic attached pronouns, on to “can / must / want”, commands, comparing, “if” and “when” — and a final lesson on how the spoken language differs from the written one. Lessons earn XP and have their own achievements.
- **Lessons** introduce 5 words at a time (you can change this). Each word comes with audio, a pronunciation breakdown and an example, then a quick quiz checks you remember them.
- **Placement check:** 24 quick questions over the first eight topics; the topics you clearly know can be marked as already learned in one tap.
- **Already learned:** tick off words you already know, during a lesson, in bulk per topic, or on a word's page. Lessons skip them, and they come back once after about a month for a quick check.
- **Spaced-repetition reviews** (an SM-2-style scheduler, like Anki) bring each word back just before you're likely to forget it.
- **Exercises get harder as you learn:** listening → reading → choosing the Turkish → flashcards → typing → speaking. The typing exercise has buttons for ç ğ ı ö ş ü. If you only get a special letter wrong, you're told exactly which one.
- **Practice mode** gives you free rounds on any topic, in any mode. It doesn't change your review schedule. It can also drill your ★ saved phrases or your **tricky words** — the ones you keep forgetting in reviews.
- **Read the script** (Japanese, Arabic, Ukrainian): see a word with no audio or reading and pick how it is read.
- **Tricky letters** (Turkish): pick how a word is read — the wrong answers are c, ç, ş, ı, ğ, ö, ü read the English way.
- **Count the beats** (Japanese): say how many beats (morae) a word has — small っ, ん and long vowels each take one.
- **Word stress** (Ukrainian, Arabic): see a word in syllables and tap the stressed one — stress is unwritten in both, and in Ukrainian phone voices sometimes misplace it. Reviews of known words ask for it now and then too.
- **Numbers by ear:** hear a number, type its digits — from 0–10 up to 10,000.
- **Dictation:** the sentence builder by ear — hear a sentence and build it from tiles; the meaning comes with the answer.
- **Hands-free listening:** a round that plays by itself — the word, a pause to say it aloud, the word again, then its meaning in English. For walking, cooking or commuting.
- **Sentence builder:** see the English, hear the sentence if you like, and put shuffled word tiles in the right order. It practises the phrases, example sentences and grammar examples (Practice → Sentences, "Practise these sentences" at the end of a grammar lesson, and now and then in reviews of phrases you know well). Japanese is built from kana tiles; Arabic reads right to left.
- **Progress:** streak, a 14-day activity chart, mastery per topic, and a searchable word list.

### Read & Listen — longer texts and speeches
- **27 graded texts per language** (A1 → B1): dialogues, short stories, everyday texts (text messages, signs, a café menu), short speeches (introducing yourself, a toast, a short talk), a set of **proverbs** people really use and a set of classic **tongue twisters** (traditional sayings, each with a word-for-word translation and a note on what it means or which sounds it trains) — plus nine **long reads** of 20–26 lines each: two folk tales (Nasreddin Hoca and Keloğlan; The Mitten and Kolobok; Momotarō and Urashima Tarō; Juha and Ali Baba), a letter to a friend, a job interview, viewing a flat to rent, and four longer speeches: a talk about a dish and how to make it (menemen, borscht, onigiri, hummus), a “Why I’m learning …” talk you can adapt and give yourself, a farewell speech to colleagues, and a short presentation about a holiday (Bayram, Ukrainian Easter, Japanese New Year, Eid al-Fitr). Arabic includes everyday Egyptian dialogues. Every line has a translation, and Japanese and Arabic lines their kana / transliteration.
- **Player:** Play all (line by line, current line highlighted), slow mode, translation shown / on tap / hidden, readings on or off, "Listen first" with the text hidden, and tap any line to hear it.
- **Tap-a-word glossary:** key words are underlined in the text; tap one for its meaning, reading and audio, and add it to your reviews.
- **Fill the gaps:** each line with one glossary word left out and four words of the text to choose from — up to ten gaps a round, different ones each time; also for your own texts.
- **Comprehension questions** with XP and achievements, synced between devices.
- **My texts:** paste your own speech or text and practise it with the same tools (line-by-line voice, listen & repeat, shadowing, recording). The app reads it as written; it doesn’t correct or translate it.
- **Speech practice:** listen & repeat line by line (scored by speech recognition, or record yourself), shadowing with the text fading out over three rounds, and recording the whole speech (1:30 to 5:00, depending on its length) to compare with the model. Dialogues have a **role-play** mode: pick a speaker and say your lines.

### Game mechanics, achievements and goals
- **XP and levels**:
  - Every answer earns XP: 10 for right, 6 for "almost", 2 for trying.
  - A combo of 5+ right answers in a row adds a bonus.
  - Bonuses for new words, finished and flawless sessions, the daily goal, speaking drills and ear training.
  - Levels come with titles: Beginner → Explorer → Word Collector → … → Polyglot.
- **48 achievements** in six groups: Learning, Habits, Pronunciation, Skill, Grammar and Reading. Locked ones show "X of Y" progress.
- **Goals**:
  - a daily goal
  - three weekly challenges, new every Monday, +100 XP each
  - a personal goal per language ("learn 100 words by …") with a projection from your recent pace
- **Streak repair:** missed a day? Do double your daily goal today to save the streak.
- **Celebrations** for level-ups, badges and goals. They wait until a session ends, and rewards that arrive together share one card.
- Everything is on the **Journey** page (under **Me**). Game features can be switched off in Settings; the daily goal, streaks and personal goals stay (the page is then called Goals).

### Sync between devices — without accounts or servers
Lisan has no backend. To copy progress to another phone, tablet or computer, go to **Settings → Sync between devices**:
1. **Create a sync code** on one device. Add a passphrase (8+ characters) to encrypt it end to end (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600k iterations → AES-GCM-256, in the browser).
2. **Move it yourself:** Copy, Share… (AirDrop, Nearby Share, messages) or Save as file to your own iCloud Drive or Google Drive.
3. **Paste it or open the file** on the other device. Check the preview, then merge.

Merging never overwrites:
- **Words:** the most recently reviewed copy of each word wins. Unmarking a word or removing a goal is kept as a tombstone, so it survives syncing.
- **Daily activity, XP and counters:** kept per device (G-counter CRDTs) and added up, so practice on two devices on the same day counts fully, and syncing in any order or repeatedly never double-counts.
- **Achievements:** combined. A reward is never paid twice.
- **Reset progress** stays on the device where you use it; **Delete all my data** (Settings) erases everything on that device.

Every code is checked before merging: malformed or absurd values are dropped, so a damaged or crafted code can't break the app.

A typical code is under 1 kB. Even every word in all four languages fits in about 20 kB.

### Pronunciation
- **Native-voice audio** at normal and slow speed, using your device's text-to-speech voices — chosen carefully:
  - **Compare voices** in Settings: hear the same sentence in every installed voice and keep the most natural one;
  - the best installed voice for the language (enhanced/neural voices first; robotic novelty voices never), and **never a voice of another language** — if none is installed you get a clear hint instead of the word read in English or Russian;
  - **Arabic** is spoken in the pausal form the transliteration shows (no unspoken case endings), and Egyptian entries use an Egyptian voice when the device has one;
  - **Japanese** words whose kanji have several readings (counters, 開く, 辛い, 明日…) are given to the voice with the right reading;
  - slow mode keeps the voice natural and adds pauses between words in phrases instead of stretching every sound.
- **Breakdown for every word:**
  - syllables, with the stressed syllable highlighted (tap any syllable to hear it alone)
  - IPA
  - an English-friendly "say it like" respelling (e.g. *merhaba* → `MEHR-hah-bah`)
  - tips on surprising letters (c, ç, ğ, ı, ö, ş, ü, j)
- **Speak & score:** say a word into the microphone. Speech recognition transcribes it, the app gives you a score, and the letters you missed are highlighted with tips. Scoring is word-aware: saying a different word (or the opposite, like *anlıyorum* for *anlamıyorum*) never counts as right, while valid spellings and homophones (多分/たぶん, hala/hâlâ, Egyptian spellings) do.
- **Record yourself** and hear your attempt played straight after the voice’s version.
- **Speak (pronunciation lab):**
  - **Alphabet:** all 29 letters with sounds and example words
  - **Sounds:** guides to ı, ö, ü, ğ, the tapped r, double consonants, word stress and vowel harmony
  - **Ear training:** minimal pairs such as *kır/kir*, *on/ön*, *cam/çam* and *eli/elli*, with a quiz
  - **Speaking drill**
  - **Practise this sound:** each sound tip about particular letters opens the speaking drill with eight course words that contain the sound, your learned words first

## Running it

```bash
npm start        # serves the website at http://localhost:8000 (no install needed)
npm test         # unit tests (Node 22+)
npm run build    # copies the web app into www/ (used by the native apps)
npm run smoke    # browser smoke test: every topic and screen in all four languages (needs Playwright)
```

Any static file server works too, e.g. `python3 -m http.server`.

**Deploying:** `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes the app to GitHub Pages on every push to `main`. Turn it on once under **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**.

## Android & iOS apps

The `android/` and `ios/` folders are native projects that wrap the web app. They use the same screens and content, but swap the browser speech APIs, which phone WebViews don't provide, for native ones:

| | Website | Android & iOS apps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audio | Web Speech API | Native text-to-speech (`@capacitor-community/text-to-speech`) |
| Speaking scores | Web Speech API | Native speech recognition (`@capacitor-community/speech-recognition`) |
| Progress storage | localStorage | localStorage, mirrored to native storage (`@capacitor/preferences`) |
| Keeping the screen on while audio plays | Screen Wake Lock API | `@capacitor-community/keep-awake` |
| Saving a backup or word list | download | share sheet (`@capacitor/filesystem` + `@capacitor/share`) |

### Getting a build

- **Android:** every push publishes an installable APK on the **[Android test build](https://github.com/ammarhijran/language/releases/tag/android-test-build)** release. Open that page on your phone, download the `.apk` and open it. You may need to allow installs from your browser or file manager. Test builds share one signing key, so each new one installs over the last and keeps your progress.
- **iOS:** every push compiles the app for the iOS Simulator to prove it builds. To install on a real iPhone, use Xcode (below) or TestFlight (see [STORE.md](STORE.md)).

### Running on your phone yourself

```bash
npm install
npm run cap:sync          # copies the web app into android/ and ios/
npm run android:open      # opens Android Studio → press Run with your phone plugged in
npm run ios:open          # opens Xcode (Mac only) → pick your iPhone → Run
```

- **Android:** needs Android Studio and JDK 21.
- **iOS:** needs a Mac with Xcode and CocoaPods. In Xcode, open *Signing & Capabilities* and select your Apple ID as the team. A free Apple ID can install on your own iPhone, but the app expires after 7 days.

Run `npm run cap:sync` again after changing anything in `js/`, `css/` or `index.html`.

## Adding a language

Each language is a pack in `js/languages/<code>/`, built with `makePack()` from `js/languages/common.js` (documented at the top of that file). The app loads only the pack being learned; names, flags and counts for the others come from the generated `js/languages/meta.js`. To add a language: create its folder, add it to `tests/all-languages.js`, and run `node scripts/lang-meta.mjs`.

A pack provides:
- `categories` (each in a `group`: words, phrases or talk) and `vocab` (with optional `reg` and `pair` for register comparisons)
- `grammar`: lessons with sections, tables, examples and a quiz
- `analyze(word)`, which returns syllables (or morae), stress, IPA and a respelling
- `normalize` / `stripDiacritics` for checking answers
- an alphabet, sound tips and minimal pairs

Optional hooks cover:
- right-to-left scripts (`dir`)
- a reading line, such as kana or transliteration (`reading`)
- romanised typing (`acceptedAnswers`)
- several spoken forms, such as kanji and kana (`spokenTargets`)

Turkish and Ukrainian sounds are derived from spelling. Japanese is derived from kana. Arabic is derived from a stored transliteration, because short vowels aren't written in everyday Arabic. The test suite checks every pack: unique ids, script, analysis, accepted answers and alphabet.

## How the content was made

All vocabulary, phrases, grammar lessons and Read & Listen texts were written and proofread with AI assistance: a drafting pass, separate checking passes, and automated tests for the things a machine can verify (stress positions, kana readings against the kanji, transliteration against the vowelled Arabic, glossary items, duplicates). **They have not yet been reviewed by native speakers.** The app says so in Settings. Everything is plain data under `js/languages/<code>/`, so corrections are easy to make.

## Notes on the Turkish content

- Stress is marked only where it is confidently known. That covers regular final stress, plus common exceptions: question words, many adverbs, place names, the days of the week, loanwords such as *lokanta* and *domates*, and the syllable before a negative suffix (*anlamıyorum*). Words whose stress is uncertain or varies are left unmarked rather than guessed.
- IPA is a broad transcription.

# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Changed
- **Sharper multiple choice:** when a word has look-alikes (one letter apart: *kış / kız / kuş*, *سَنَة / سِتَّة*…), up to two of them are among the wrong answers — in listening, reading and “choose the word” questions — instead of unrelated words from the topic. Telling those apart is what the question should test.
- **Privacy policy:** now lists the words, phrases and texts you add yourself among what is stored on the device, and says that sync codes carry your own words (My words) and your notes on words besides your progress — never your own texts or settings. The sync screen’s “not encrypted” note says the same.
- **Smaller, faster-loading language packs in the built app:** the word lists, lessons, texts and readings stay readable (indented) in the repository, and the build writes them compactly — the same values, checked file by file — which makes the packs about 20 % smaller (around 650 kB in total).
- **Practice screen:** the ten modes are compact cards (icon and name) with one line below describing the selected mode, so “What to practise” and Start are reached with much less scrolling.

### Added
- **Fixed:** with the largest text size on a narrow phone, the Play / Pause label in a text’s player bar was cut off at both ends; the row now wraps instead.
- **Continue where you stopped** (Read & Listen): a text left part-way remembers the line; opening it again offers “Continue from line 14 of 32”. Read to the end, it starts from the top next time.
- **Hands-free, keep going** (Practice → Hands-free → Options): when a round ends, the next one starts by itself with a fresh draw of words, up to eight rounds in a row, for a walk or a drive.
- **Hands-free, meaning first** (Practice → Hands-free → Options): the English is said first, then a pause to say the word yourself, then the word — audio flashcards, for recalling words with your hands and eyes busy. The word on screen stays covered until it is said.
- **Tidier Alphabet tab:** its quizzes are one row of chips under “Quiz yourself”, instead of a stack of buttons that had grown to six for Japanese.
- **Rule quizzes on the Practice screen:** each language’s pronunciation-rule quiz (vowel harmony, я ю є, は, sun and moon letters) has its own link under “Speak & listen”, next to Numbers by ear.
- **は: ha or wa?** (Speak → Kana, Japanese): ten of the course’s example sentences a round, shown in kana word by word with one は or へ marked — is it *wa* or *ha*, *e* or *he*? On its own it is a particle (wa, e); inside a word it is ha, he. Each answer says which, and plays the sentence.
- **я, ю, є: two sounds or one?** (Speak → Alphabet, Ukrainian): ten course words a round with one of the three letters marked — is it *ya* or *a*, *yu* or *u*, *ye* or *e*? Two sounds at the start of a word, after a vowel and after an apostrophe; one after a consonant, which becomes soft. Each answer says which and why, and plays the word.
- **Vowel harmony** (Speak → Alphabet, Turkish): ten everyday nouns a round — which plural, *-lar* or *-ler*? which question word, *mı, mi, mu* or *mü*? which “in / at”, *-da, -de, -ta* or *-te*? The ending copies the word’s last vowel; each answer names that vowel and says the result aloud. Only words that follow the rule are asked.
- **Sun or moon letter?** (Speak → Alphabet, Arabic): ten letters a round — “the” before a word that begins with this letter: is it *ash-sh…* or *al-sh…*? Before the fourteen sun letters the l of al- is not said and the letter is doubled; before the others it is said in full. Each answer says which it is and why; the round is half sun, half moon. A few questions in each round are real course words that begin with the article (السَّبْت — *as-sabt*), played and shown in full after the answer.
- **Two more achievements** (54 in all): Reads the Script (100 letters right in the letter quiz) and Good with Numbers (50 numbers right by ear).
- **Copy a topic** (a topic’s page → Copy): its words or phrases as text, one a line with reading and meaning.
- **Flashcards the other way round** (Settings → Lessons → “Flashcards show first”): the word itself can be the front of the card — you see and hear it, and recall what it means — instead of the meaning.
- **Practise these again:** when a review or a round ends with four or more missed words, the summary’s “Worth another look” list has a button that starts a mixed round of just those words, and one that plays them hands-free.
- **Learn it again from the start** (a learned word’s page): takes a word that never stuck back among the new words, after asking — its lesson then teaches it again.
- **Search → My words:** when a search finds no word, “Add it to My words” is one tap away — with the word filled in when you typed it in the language’s own script.
- **Go on by itself after a right answer** (Settings → Lessons; off by default): in multiple-choice questions, a right answer moves on to the next question once the word has been said — one tap a question instead of two. A wrong answer always waits for you.
- **Review ahead** (the Review screen, when nothing is due): the words that come up by the end of tomorrow can be reviewed today — for the evening before a day without time. They are scheduled from today, like any review.
- **Copy My words** (My words → “Copy the list”): all your own words as text, one a line — word, meaning, reading, note. It pastes into a spreadsheet as columns, and into “Paste a list” on another device as the same words.
- **Sleep timer** (Read & Listen, in a text’s player bar): ⏱ 10, 20, 30 or 60 minutes — when the time is up, playing stops, and Keep playing does not go on to the next text. The timer carries on from text to text and shows the minutes left.
- **Speed in the reader** (Read & Listen, in a text’s player bar): the voice’s speed can be changed while listening — 0.6× to 1.1× — and the next line is read with it. It is the same speaking speed as in Settings → Audio.
- **Two more word-list filters** (Progress → Word list): “Missed before”, the words you have got wrong in reviews with the most-missed first, and “With a note of mine”.
- **A word’s record** (a word’s page): one line under the review date — how many times in a row you have answered it right, how often you missed it, and how far apart its reviews now are.
- **Copy or share your saved phrases** (Phrasebook → ★ Saved): the whole list as text, one phrase a line with its reading and meaning — for a notes app, a message to yourself, or paper before a trip.
- **Letter quiz** (Speak → Alphabet; Japanese, Arabic, Ukrainian): ten letters a round — a letter is shown and you pick how it is read, or the reading is shown and you pick the letter. Japanese has five quizzes — hiragana, katakana, the voiced kana (が, ぱ…) and the combined kana (きゃ, しゅ…), answered in rōmaji, and “Hiragana ↔ katakana”, where a kana is shown in one script and picked in the other; Arabic letters are asked by name (alif, bāʾ…), and a second Arabic quiz, “Shapes in a word”, asks them in their joined shapes — at the start, in the middle and at the end of a word; Ukrainian letters by their usual Latin spelling. About one question in three is by ear, where a letter’s example word begins with it: the word is played and you pick the letter it starts with. Up to two of the wrong answers are close to the right one (か next to き and さ). The answer shows the letter’s sound, the note from the chart and its example word with audio; the letters you missed are listed at the end and come up again in the next round. Keys 1–4 answer, Enter goes on. Near the start of a course in a new script, Home has a “New script?” row that opens the chart and its quizzes.
- **Your skills** (Progress): every answer in a lesson, a review or a practice round is now filed under what its question trains — Listening, Reading, Recall, Spelling, Speaking, Sentences, Sounds, Letters, Numbers — and Progress shows how the last answers of each kind went (up to 40), side by side. When one is clearly behind the others, the card says so and offers a round of just that. A kind appears after eight answers, the card once there are two to compare; a tap taken back with “I mis-tapped” is not counted. Numbers by ear and the letter quiz have rows of their own (Numbers, Letters). When a skill is behind, the Practice screen also offers it right under the review card (“Work on spelling”). Kept on this device and in backup files; resetting a language’s progress clears it.
- **Voice says it wrong?** (on every word’s page, under the pronunciation box): phone voices differ, and each gets its own words wrong. Here you can spell a word for the voice only — in kana, with an accent mark, with a hyphen between syllables — listen to it before keeping it, and go back to the course’s spelling at any time. The word’s example sentence has the same option (“Voice reads the sentence wrong?”). So does every line of a text in Read & Listen: 🔧 in the player bar respells the line being read. The word on screen, its reading and the answers that count stay the same; your spelling is used wherever the word is spoken (lessons, reviews, hands-free). It is kept on this device and in backup files, not in sync codes, since another device has another voice. Settings → Audio lists your spellings, each a link to its word, with one button to undo them all.
- **Years and phone numbers by ear** (Practice → Numbers by ear → “In real life”): two new rounds. *Years* says ten years between 1950 and 2030. *Phone numbers* says seven digits one at a time — each digit on its own, so no voice can run them together into one big number — and you type them; a leading 0 counts, spaces and hyphens do not. A wrong answer shows the number in groups with the digits you typed differently marked.
- **Read words from their kana** (Settings → Audio, Japanese; off by default): a kanji word can have more than one reading, and a phone voice sometimes picks the wrong one. With the switch on, every single-word entry is sent to the voice in kana (今日 as きょう), so the reading is always the course’s. A voice may give a word a slightly different melody from kana, so it is a switch to try: toggling it says a sample word at once, and a button under it plays the sample again. Phrases, example sentences and texts are still sent as written — without kanji a voice loses where the words end — and so are one-kana words such as 歯 (は), which alone could be taken for a particle.
- **Mark the stress for the voice** (Settings → Audio, Ukrainian; off by default): phone voices sometimes stress a Ukrainian word on the wrong syllable, and the course knows the stress of its entries. With the switch on, an entry is sent to the voice with an acute accent on its stressed vowel (молоко́) — the way dictionaries print stress, which some voices read it from. Others ignore the mark or stumble over it, so it is a switch to try: toggling it says a sample word at once. Example sentences and texts, which carry no stress data, are sent as written.
- **Tips** (Me → Tips): a short guide to what the app can do and where each thing is — lessons and reviews, the practice modes, pronunciation, Read & Listen, My words and notes, themes, backup and sync — one line each, with a link that opens it. It names the language’s own sound exercise and leaves out what does not apply (no reminder tip on the website). Search finds the tips too: typing “dark”, “backup” or “stress” shows the feature under “In the app”.
- **Four new achievements** (52 in all): In Context (30 words put back into their sentences), Sound Sense (50 stress, beats or tricky-letter questions right), Word Collector (learn 10 words you added yourself) and Memory Hooks (your own note on 10 words).
- **Share** on word and phrase pages (where the device has a share sheet): sends the word with its reading and meaning — “水 (みず · mizu) — water” — to a chat, mail or notes app.
- **Keep playing** (Read & Listen, in the player bar; “⏭ Next” on narrow screens): when a text ends, the next one in the list opens and starts by itself — the stories and speeches become one long listening session. It stops after the last text, and the switch is remembered.
- **Last 6 weeks** (Progress): a calendar of days, Monday first — the more you did, the darker the day; the darkest are days the daily goal was met, today is outlined. Below it: on how many days you practised and met the goal.
- **When the next reviews come:** with nothing due, the Review screen and the Practice card now say how many words come back and when — “Next: 7 words tomorrow”, “3 words on Thursday” — instead of only a date or “check back later”.
- **Hands-free listening can add the example sentence** (Practice → Hands-free → Options → “Then say its example sentence too”): after each word you hear its sentence, get a pause to repeat it and — with the English option — hear what it means. A twenty-word round becomes several minutes of listening made of sentences that are already part of the course.
- **Build a text’s sentences, and take it as dictation** (Read & Listen, under each text): two new links — “Build its sentences” (the translation is shown; put the words of a line in order) and “Dictation” (hear a line, build it) — make rounds of up to ten of the text’s own lines. Lines too short or too long to build are left out; almost every text has enough.
- **Typing and speaking questions can be switched off** (Settings → Lessons): for reviews and Mixed practice — when there is no keyboard for the language, or no way to talk. The words are then asked by choosing or as flashcards. Spelling and Speaking practice stay available when picked on purpose.
- **In context** (Practice → In context; also in Mixed rounds and in reviews of words past their first steps): the word’s own example sentence is shown with a gap where the word goes, with its translation, and you choose the word; the answer shows and plays the whole sentence. Asked only when the sentence has the word exactly as the entry writes it, once — 533 Turkish, 613 Ukrainian, 719 Japanese and 248 Arabic entries.
- **Copy** on every word and phrase page: puts the word on the clipboard, to paste into a message, a search or a note.
- **My note** also shows on the back of flashcards and on new-word cards, not only under multiple-choice and typed answers.
- **Ear training with the course’s own words:** besides the hand-picked pairs, Speak → Ear training now has a group “From the course: words one sound apart” — twelve pairs such as *kış / kuş*, *так / там*, *وَلَد / بَلَد*, the ones you have learned first — and the quiz draws from them too. For Japanese the pairs that matter most come first: voiced against unvoiced (あける / あげる) and words one beat longer (a long vowel, っ or ん).
- **Easy to mix up** (a word’s page): the other entries that are spelled or read almost the same — one letter apart — are listed with their meanings: *kış* (winter) next to *kız* (girl) and *kuş* (bird), おばさん next to おばあさん. Compared in kana for Japanese and in transliteration for Arabic.
- **Speak → Sounds** starts with a shortcut to the language’s own sound exercise: Tricky letters (Turkish), Word stress (Ukrainian, Arabic) or Count the beats (Japanese).
- **Tricky letters** (Practice, Turkish; also now and then in Mixed rounds): a word is shown with no audio and you pick how it is read. The wrong answers are what it would sound like with c, ç, ş, ı, ğ, ö, ü or j read the English way — *çocuk*: choh-JOOK, not koh-KOOK or choh-SOOK — and the answer says what each of those letters is. 370 one-word entries; words with two or more ways to misread them come first.
- **Reviews coming up** (Progress): a seven-day chart of how many words fall due on each day — today’s bar includes anything overdue — so a heavy day is visible before it arrives.
- **Worth another look:** the summary after a review or a practice round lists the words you missed, each a link to its page — to hear it again, or to add a note of your own. (A wrong tap you marked as a slip is not listed.)
- **My note** on any course word or phrase (its page → “📝 Add a note of my own”): a memory hook, where you heard it, a sentence of your own — up to 300 characters. It is shown on the word’s page and under every answer about that word, kept in backups and merged by sync (the newest version wins; removing it on one device removes it on the other), and it survives “Reset progress”. Search finds words by what you wrote about them (“In your notes”), and “Export my words (CSV)” has a “My note” column.
- **“I mis-tapped — I knew this one”** on the wrong-answer sheet of multiple-choice questions: the word is not treated as forgotten — in a review it counts as Hard (it comes back sooner than after Good, without starting over and without lowering its ease). The points and the combo of that answer stay as they fell.
- **A reminder to keep a copy** (Me, and Settings → Your data): everything is stored on the device, so once you have learned 30 words and no backup file or sync code has left this device for a month (or ever), Me shows a note with “Export a backup” and “Make a sync code” — “Not now” puts it off for a month. Settings → Your data says when the last backup or sync code was made. Nothing is sent anywhere; the app only remembers the date.
- **My words** (Learn → Words → “Add your own words”): add words and phrases you met elsewhere — the word, its meaning, an optional note and, for Japanese and Arabic, an optional reading (kana / transliteration). They become one more topic, “📝 My words”, so lessons, spaced-repetition reviews, every practice mode, hands-free listening, search, the word page, ★ and the CSV export work for them exactly like for the course’s own entries; they can be changed or deleted at any time (deleting also forgets their progress). A word that is already in the course or in your list is pointed out instead of being added twice. Up to 300 per language, stored on the device, in backup files and in sync codes: syncing two devices combines their lists (for a word both have, the copy changed last wins, and a deletion on one device removes it on the other). They are not part of the course: course-completion achievements and topic counts ignore them. Japanese words with kanji and Arabic words without a reading are shown in one piece (no syllables or rōmaji).
- **My words — add several at once:** paste or type a list, one entry per line (“word – meaning”, “word = meaning”, “word; meaning”, or two columns copied from a spreadsheet; for Japanese and Arabic the reading can follow the word in brackets or be a third column). Entries that are already in the course or in your list, and lines without a meaning, are named and left in the box to correct.
- **My words — stress for Ukrainian:** the form shows the word in syllables and you tap the stressed one of each word (optional). Accent marks typed or pasted with a word, as dictionaries print them (мете́лик), are read as its stress, also in a pasted list. Marked words show their stress on the word page and in the “say it like” line, and join Word stress practice and the stress questions in reviews.
- **Words to add from your own text** (Read & Listen → My texts; Turkish, Ukrainian, Arabic): under a text you pasted, a “🆕 Words to add” card lists its words that are in none of your word lists as written (up to 60, in order). Each can be listened to and opens the My words form with the word filled in — you type the meaning. Words you add disappear from the list. (Inflected forms of course words are listed too, because only the written form can be compared; Japanese has no spaces between words, so it has no such list.)
- **Home → Your words:** while some of your own words are still new, Home shows “📝 My words · n to learn”, one tap from a lesson on them. The course’s own “Up next” never suggests them.
- **Phrasebook → 📝 Mine:** your own words and phrases have a chip in the phrasebook, next to ★ Saved and Trip essentials — with audio, ☆, Show mode (big letters) and links to practise them; the phrasebook’s search finds them too.
- **Add to My words from a text** (Read & Listen): the glossary sheet of a word that is not one of the course’s entries — about 300 per language — has “＋ Add to My words”; the word is kept with its meaning, its reading (kana / transliteration) and the title of the text it came from, and can go straight into your reviews.
- **Count the beats** (Practice, Japanese): a word is shown in its script and in kana, with no audio, and you say how many beats (morae) it has — 444 one-word entries where counting teaches something: a small っ, ん, a long vowel, a contracted sound such as しゃ. The answer shows the word beat by beat (しゃ · し · ん — 3 beats) and says it. Japanese has no stress; giving every beat the same length is what makes it sound right.
- **Mixed practice asks how words sound:** a Mixed round now includes a stress question (Ukrainian, Arabic) or a beats question (Japanese) now and then, for the words that have one.
- **Arabic stress answers say why:** after a stress question the answer names the rule that applied — an extra-heavy last syllable, a heavy second-to-last one, otherwise the third from the end (or the first of two), the article never stressed, and Egyptian’s own counting — so the practice teaches the pattern, not 650 separate words. The same sentence is on each Arabic word’s page, under its syllables.
- **Word stress for Arabic too** (Practice → Word stress, and now and then in reviews of words you know): the word is shown in Arabic and you tap the stressed syllable of its transliteration — 650 one-word entries, Standard and Egyptian each by its own rule (كِتَاب ki-TĀB, شَجَرَة SHA-ja-ra).
- **Word stress** (Practice, Ukrainian): a word is shown split into syllables, with no audio and no reading, and you tap the syllable that is stressed; the answer then shows and says it. Ukrainian stress is free and unwritten, and phone voices do not always get it right — this trains it from the course’s own stress marks (696 one-word entries of two or more syllables). Reviews of words you already know also ask for the stress now and then; a miss there counts as “Hard” (the word comes back sooner) rather than as a forgotten word.
- **Status-bar icons follow a forced theme** (Android with WebView 140 or newer, and iOS): with Theme set to Light or Dark while the phone is the other way round, the clock and battery icons switch to match the app, through Capacitor’s built-in SystemBars plugin — no new dependency. Older Android WebViews keep the phone’s own strip and icons.
- **Themes** (Settings → Display): **Theme** — Same as device, Light or Dark; **Colour** — the colour of buttons, links and highlights: Indigo, Blue, Teal, Green, Orange, Rose, Purple, or *your own* (a hue slider; the app picks a shade of that hue that stays readable); **Background** — Warm, Grey, Cool, Paper, or White / true Black. Changes apply at once and are used from the very first frame the next time the app opens (the loading screen included). The dark theme moved to its own stylesheet (`css/dark.css`) whose media the app switches, so there is still one set of dark rules; right / wrong colours and the brand red do not change. Tests check every colour (presets and 72 hues) against every background in both modes for WCAG AA contrast.
- **Search inside the texts:** Search now also finds sentences in the Read & Listen texts — a proverb, a line of a dialogue, a step of a recipe — by its words in the language (Arabic without vowel marks, any capitalisation) or by its translation, and opens the text it is in. Up to eight lines, at most two per text.
- **Ten more topics per language (67 → 77), 144 new entries each:** six word topics — Housework & Repairs, Cars & Driving, Holidays & Celebrations, News & Media, Landscape & Environment, More Describing Words (16 words each, every one with an example sentence) — and four phrase situations — Sightseeing & Tickets, Dates, Times & Appointments, Hairdresser, Repairs & Services, Getting to Know People (12 phrases each, most with a polite ⇄ everyday pair; in Arabic the everyday form is Egyptian). They form a fourth stage on the Learn screen, “Wider world”. The new first-person phrases have their feminine forms for Ukrainian and Arabic (“I speak as a woman”).
- **Practise this sound** (Speak → Sounds): under each sound tip that is about particular letters — Turkish ı, ö, ü, ğ, double consonants; Ukrainian г, soft consonants, the apostrophe, в as “w”, voiced endings; Japanese long vowels, small っ, the r, し / ち / つ / ふ, whispered vowels; Arabic emphatics, ع / ح, خ / غ, ق, ث / ذ, long vowels, shadda, the article — a button opens the speaking drill with eight words of the course that contain it, the ones you have already learned first. The words are found from their spelling (the kana for Japanese). Tips about rhythm, stress or pitch have no drill.
- **Three more long reads per language (24 → 27 texts):** a second folk tale (Keloğlan and the jar of gold, Kolobok, Urashima Tarō, Ali Baba), a long practical dialogue — viewing a flat to rent (rooms, rent, deposit, bills, when to move in) — and a talk a learner can give: a dish and how to make it, step by step (menemen, borscht, onigiri, hummus). 23–26 lines each, with a 12–18-word glossary, questions, role-play for the dialogue and speech practice for the talk.
- **Fill the gaps** (Read & Listen, under every text’s glossary and after its questions): each line appears with one glossary word left out and four words of the text to choose from, with the translation as a hint — up to ten gaps a round, different ones each time. After each answer the line is read aloud and the word’s meaning is shown. Works for your own texts too (with the course’s words found in them). Made from the texts’ existing glossaries, so there is no new content to check.
- **Four more grammar lessons per language (12 → 16):** Turkish — can / must / want (-ebil, -meli, lazım, istemek), commands and suggestions (gel, gelin, gidelim, -eyim mi?), comparing and linking (daha, en, kadar, gibi, çünkü, ile, için), and if / when / before / after (-se, -ince, -ken, -meden önce, -dikten sonra). Ukrainian — the dative (мені подобається, мені треба, мені … років), verbs of motion (іти / їхати, ходити / їздити, prefixes), the imperative (читай / читайте, не + imperfective, давай, хай), and comparing (-іший, най-, ніж / за / від). Japanese — reasons and linking (から, ので, けど, て), can / may / must (potential form, てもいい, てはいけません, なければなりません), giving and receiving (あげる, くれる, もらう, てくれませんか), and if / when (たら, と, ば, なら, とき). Arabic — the future and “want to / must / can” (سَـ, لَنْ, أُرِيدُ أَنْ, يَجِبُ أَنْ), commands and requests, كَانَ (was, had, used to), and comparing (أَفْعَل, أَكْثَر / أَقَلّ), each with the everyday Egyptian equivalents. About 240 new playable example sentences, which also feed the “build the sentence” exercise. Written and proof-read with AI assistance, like the other lessons.
- **Proverbs and tongue twisters** (Read & Listen, two new kinds of text; also linked from Speak → Sounds and from the Phrasebook): for each language a set of proverbs people really use (12–13) and a set of classic tongue twisters (8–9; five for Arabic, including two famous lines of classical Arabic). All are traditional sayings, not written for the app. Each line has a word-for-word translation and a note under it — what the proverb means, or which sounds the twister trains — and both sets can be practised line by line with Listen & repeat (scored) and Shadowing. In Japanese the voice is given the traditional reading where it could misread the kanji (生麦生米生卵, 十人十色, 笑う門には福来る…).
- **Export my words (CSV)** (Settings → Your data): the words you have started in the current language as a spreadsheet file — word, reading, meaning, example, topic and status — for printing or a flashcard program.
- **Placement check** (Learn → Already know some words? → “Not sure what you know?”, and from the last welcome step): 24 quick questions, three from each of the first eight topics. Topics where all three were right can be marked as already learned in one tap (untick any you would rather go through); those words still come back once, a month later, for a quick check.
- **Getting started:** for Ukrainian and Arabic the welcome steps ask “How do you speak?” (as a man / as a woman), so the right first-person forms are used from the first lesson; and the last step offers “Travelling soon? Start with the trip essentials” as an alternative first lesson.
- **Read the script** (Practice, for Japanese, Arabic and Ukrainian): a word is shown in its script with no audio and no reading, and you pick how it is read from four options of similar length — rōmaji, the transliteration, or the “say it like” respelling. For getting off the reading aids.
- **More ear training:** about twice as many minimal pairs for Ukrainian, Japanese and Arabic (20 each), and five more for Turkish (24) — new contrasts such as Ukrainian г / х and hard / soft consonants, Japanese k / g and t / d (てんき / でんき), Arabic د / ض and غ / خ. Every pair is checked to differ in exactly one sound.
- **Numbers by ear** (Practice → Numbers by ear): the voice says a number and you type its digits — four ranges from 0–10 up to 10,000, ten numbers a round. The answer also shows the course’s word for the number where it has one.
- **Four new achievements** (48 in all): Easy Listening (5 hands-free rounds), Sharp Ears (20 sentences built by ear), In My Own Words (practise a text of your own) and Ready to Travel (learn all the trip essentials of a language).
- **Home:** near the start of a language, a “Trip soon? 🎒 Essentials” row leads straight to the trip-essentials crash course.
